## How it fits together

- The header is the section's title and a subtitle line your app writes, such as "Updated just now" or "Updating…". When the subtitle changes, the new line fades in over the old one.
- The headline metric sits on a plain card in a `ScrubChart`. The chart owns everything in it: the uppercase date line, the figure with its decimals dimmed, the tinted change chip, the line you drag across to read any day, the hold that pins a range and shows its change, and the range picker of joined glass segments. The block gives it one series per range and a binding to the picked range, and names the metric above it.
- The supporting numbers are `LiveStat` tiles in a grid below the card. Each tile rolls its figure when its value changes, scrubs its sparkline under a held finger, and opens into a taller chart with its low and high when tapped. The block keeps one tile open at a time, and an open tile takes the full width so its low and high fit on one line.
- Two tiles share a row only when both fit in half of it. The grid measures what each tile's label and change chip need on one line and gives a tile the whole row when half is too narrow, so no label is cut short and no chip wraps. A tile left without a partner also takes the whole row. On a 390 pt wide screen, labels of up to about eight letters with a whole-number change sit two to a row.
- While `phase` is `.loading`, the same layout is drawn as `SkeletonLoader` bones: the card at the height the chart will take, with bones for the name, the figure, the plot and the picker, and a shell for each tile to come. When the numbers arrive the bones fade out and the pieces make their own entrance: the chart unfolds its line and the tiles count up as their lines draw on.
- `.failed(message)` replaces the numbers with your message and a glass retry button. The button runs `onRetry`, shows progress while it runs and ignores taps until it returns.

## Wiring it to your app

The block takes values, not a data source. Keep the headline, the stats and the phase in your model, and the block follows them: a new value rolls a tile's figure, new points bend the chart's line in place, and a new phase crosses between bones, numbers and the failed state.

`Format` picks how a number reads, for the chart and for each tile: `.number` (12,480), `.percent` from a fraction (0.033 reads 3.3%), or `.currency(code:)`. `delta` is a fraction too: 0.08 shows as 8%. `history` is the tile's sparkline, oldest first. Each stat keeps its place by `id`, which defaults to its label.

```swift
@Observable final class OverviewModel {
    var phase: StatsOverviewBlock.Phase = .loading
    var revenue: [StatsOverviewBlock.Series] = []
    var stats: [StatsOverviewBlock.Stat] = []
    var updated = "Updating…"

    func load() async {
        // Bones only for the first load; a refresh keeps the numbers on screen.
        if stats.isEmpty { phase = .loading }
        updated = "Updating…"
        do {
            let report = try await API.overview()
            revenue = report.ranges.map { range in
                .init(label: range.label, points: range.days.map { .init(date: $0.date, value: $0.total) })
            }
            stats = [
                .init(label: "Visitors", value: report.visitors, delta: report.visitorsChange, history: report.visitorsByDay),
                .init(label: "Conversion", value: report.conversion, format: .percent, delta: report.conversionChange, history: report.conversionByDay),
            ]
            updated = "Updated just now"
            phase = .loaded
        } catch {
            phase = .failed("Couldn't load your numbers. Check your connection and try again.")
        }
    }
}

struct DashboardView: View {
    @State private var model = OverviewModel()
    @State private var range = 1

    var body: some View {
        ScrollView {
            StatsOverviewBlock(
                subtitle: model.updated,
                headline: .init(label: "Recurring revenue", format: .currency(code: "USD"), series: model.revenue),
                stats: model.stats,
                phase: model.phase,
                range: $range,
                onRetry: { await model.load() }
            )
            .padding(20)
        }
        .refreshable { await model.load() }
        .task { await model.load() }
    }
}
```

Pass `range` when your app needs to know the picked range, for example to fetch the 90-day series only when someone asks for it: give that range an empty series until it arrives, and the chart shows its own "No data yet" state and unfolds the line when the points land. A refresh that keeps the numbers on screen while it works, like the pull to refresh above, leaves `phase` at `.loaded` and only changes the subtitle.

## Customizing

- `title` defaults to "Overview". The headline's `label` names the metric on the card.
- `Style` holds the header's `text` and `muted` colors, the `card` surface and its `cornerRadius`, and the pieces' own styles: `chart` (`ScrubChartStyle`), `stat` (`LiveStatStyle`) and `bones` (`SkeletonLoader.Style`). Keep `chart.ground` the same color as `card`, since the chart rings its dots in it.
- The standard style sets the chart's figure at 40 pt and the tiles' figures at 30 pt, sized for a half-width tile. Raise `stat.numeralSize` if your tiles usually take the whole row.
- The plot's height is `plot` in `StatsOverviewChartSlot`. The grid's spacing is `gap`. The rule for sharing a row is in `StatsOverviewGrid.rows`.
- To show a stat with fixed decimals or in another unit, add a case to `Format` and map it to a `FormatStyle` in `chart` and `tile(_:)`.

## Accessibility

- The section title and the metric's name are headers. VoiceOver reads the chart as one element with its latest value and change; swiping up or down steps through the days, and the actions rotor offers the other ranges ("Show 7D", "Show 90D"), since the chart's own picker sits inside that element. Each tile reads its label and value, can be swiped through its history, and opens or closes with a double tap.
- While loading, the bones read as one element, "Loading Overview". The failed state reads your message, then the retry button, which reads "Trying again" while it runs.
- Text grows with Dynamic Type. At the accessibility sizes the tiles stack one to a row. The chart's text stops growing at the largest standard size, because its figure and change chip share one line and the figure would otherwise be cut short; the rest of the block keeps growing.
- Reduce Motion and Reduce Transparency are handled by the pieces: the chart's line appears in place instead of unfolding and its figures crossfade instead of rolling, the tiles' figures settle in a quarter second, the bones breathe instead of sweeping, and glass draws as solid fills. The block's own changes of phase are crossfades.
