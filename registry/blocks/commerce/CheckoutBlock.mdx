## How it fits together

- `StepTrail` sits at the top with three steps: Bag, Delivery and Pay. Its selection is the step on screen and its `reached` is the furthest step people have moved on to, cut short at any step that is no longer complete. Tapping a done step goes back to it, and a step that has been reached can be returned to forward. Its accent is set to the label colour, so the signal red stays on the one primary action below.
- The content under the trail changes with the step. The step leaving slides a little the way the trail moved, blurs out and gives its height back as it goes, while the next one blurs in from the other side, so the section grows or shrinks with it rather than after it. The Continue and Pay buttons sit at the end of each step, so they travel with what they act on.
- Bag: each line is a drawn tile (an SF Symbol in ink on a house colour, never an image), its name and variant, its price level with its name, and a `ScrubStepper` for the quantity, from zero to the line's `maximumQuantity`. The steppers are neutral glass. Once there are several, the unit price shows beside the variant. The subtotal rolls to its new value as quantities change.
- A line taken to zero dims, and once the stepper has rested there for a moment it turns into a removed row with an Undo chip, which brings back the quantity it had. Removed lines stay in the list until people leave the bag, and then they are dropped from `checkout.items`. A bag with nothing in it shows an empty state and keeps Continue disabled.
- Delivery: an `AddressField` for the address and a card of delivery speeds, each a row with a radio mark, its arrival estimate and its price (Free at zero). Continue turns on once an address has resolved and a speed is chosen. An address used as typed counts; the field marks it as unverified.
- Pay: one card holds where the order goes (the address and the chosen speed), the payment method your app provides with a drawn mark for its kind, and the totals: subtotal, delivery, tax when you give a rate, and the total. Under it, a `CommitButton` reads "Pay" and the total. A tap draws the capsule in around its label while a ring buds out beside it and `onPay` runs.
- On success the ring closes on a check and the label turns to Paid, then the trail completes and the section shows the placed order with a sage check. On failure the capsule turns butter, reads Payment failed and offers a retry, and the reason shows under it. The trail is locked while a payment is out and once the order is placed.
- Continue and Pay are `CommitButton`s, so a disabled step reads as clear glass with a grey label, and every press has the button's own sink and haptic.

## Wiring it to your app

The block takes one value your app owns, `CheckoutBlock.Checkout`, and one closure, `onPay`. The block writes quantities, the address and the delivery choice back to the binding, so your app always sees the bag as it stands. `onPay` gets a `CheckoutBlock.Order`: the lines with a quantity above zero, the address, the chosen speed, the payment method and the amounts, exactly as the Pay step showed them. Return when the payment went through; throw to show the failure. A `LocalizedError`'s `errorDescription` is the reason shown under the button.

```swift
@State private var checkout = CheckoutBlock.Checkout(
    items: bag.lines.map { line in
        CheckoutBlock.LineItem(id: line.sku, name: line.name, variant: line.variant, unitPrice: line.price, quantity: line.quantity, thumbnail: .init(symbol: "mug.fill", tile: .sage))
    },
    deliveryOptions: [
        .init(title: "Standard", estimate: "In 4–5 days", price: 0),
        .init(title: "Express", estimate: "Arrives tomorrow", price: 8),
    ],
    deliveryID: "Standard",
    payment: .init(title: "Visa ending 4242", detail: "Expires 08/28"),
    currencyCode: "USD",
    taxRate: Decimal(string: "0.0825")
)

ScrollView {
    CheckoutBlock(checkout: $checkout, countries: ["US"], onChangePayment: { showsPaymentPicker = true }) { order in
        try await store.charge(order.total, currency: order.currencyCode, shipTo: order.address)
    }
    .padding(20)
}
```

- Money is `Decimal` throughout. Amounts format in the person's locale from `currencyCode`, so "USD" reads $64.00 in the United States and 64,00 $US in France. Tax is the subtotal times `taxRate`, rounded half up to the currency's minor unit; leave `taxRate` nil to show no tax line, or fold your own tax into the prices.
- Address suggestions come from MapKit by default. That needs no location permission and no Info.plist key: suggestions come from what is typed, optionally biased by `addressRegion`. Pass `countries` to keep to the places you deliver to, or `addressSource` to use your own geocoding backend, or a fixed list in previews and tests.
- To start from a saved address, set `checkout.address`; the field fills itself in. Setting `checkout.deliveryID` preselects a speed. With no `deliveryOptions`, Delivery only asks for the address.
- `onChangePayment` shows a Change chip on the payment row. Present your own picker and set `checkout.payment`. Leave it nil to hide the chip.
- After a successful payment the block shows the placed order and stays there. Dismiss the sheet or move on from your own code once `onPay` has returned.

## Customizing

- The colours are in `CheckoutPalette` near the end of the file: the ground, the card fill, the label greys, the hairline and the house blocks used for tiles. The trail's monochrome accent is set where `StepTrail` is created; the Pay and Continue buttons use `CommitButton`'s house style.
- Copy goes through `String(localized:)`, so it can be translated in your String Catalog. The step titles are in `titles`, at the top of `CheckoutBlock`.
- `Thumbnail.Tile.custom(_:)` takes any colour for a tile; pick one light enough for ink to read on it. A different SF Symbol per product is usually enough to tell lines apart.
- `PaymentMethod.Kind` picks the drawn mark: a small card, a wallet glyph or a bank glyph. The block never draws a network or wallet logo.
- The removed row waits 0.8 seconds at zero before it turns into Undo. The value is in `CheckoutLineRow`, beside the comment about a finger still on the stepper.
- The steps are a `switch` in `content(for:)`. To add a step, add its title, its view and its completeness check to `reachable`.

## Accessibility

- VoiceOver reads the trail as "Checkout progress" with the step as its value, and it can be adjusted to move between the steps that are reached. Each line reads as one element (name, variant and price), followed by its stepper, labelled with the line's name, which adjusts the quantity.
- A line removed at zero is announced, and its Undo chip carries a hint naming what it puts back. The delivery rows are buttons that read their name, estimate and price, with the selected one marked selected. The totals read as label and amount pairs.
- The Pay button reads its title and its state (loading, Paid or the failure), and "Order placed" is announced when the order goes through.
- Every type size up to the largest accessibility sizes fits without clipping: at accessibility sizes the line tiles and the address and payment marks step aside, prices move under the names, the Change chip moves under the payment method, and the totals stack their amounts under their labels.
- Reduce Motion cross-fades the steps in place instead of sliding and blurring them, and the radio and placed check fade rather than scale. Reduce Transparency draws the glass parts as solid shapes. Light and dark each have their own values for every colour.
