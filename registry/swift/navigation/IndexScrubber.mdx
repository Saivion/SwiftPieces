## Notes

- `.indexScrubber(titles, proxy: proxy)` on a `ScrollView` or `List` inside a `ScrollViewReader` is the whole setup. Give each section an id equal to its title (`ForEach(sections, id: \.title) { Section { … } }`). Jumps scroll without animation, the section's top at `anchor` (`.top` by default), the way the system index does.
- The modifier insets the scroll view's content by the rail's width (44pt at the default text size), so rows lay out beside the rail instead of under it. With no reachable sections the rail takes no space and no touches.
- Use `IndexScrubber(titles, selection: $current) { title in … }` when you scroll some other way; place it on the trailing edge yourself. `onSelect` fires once on touch down, then only when the finger reaches a new section, plus on VoiceOver and keyboard moves.
- `index: .sections` shows your titles as they are. `.alphabet` shows the current locale's index letters (A to Z and # in English, from `UILocalizedIndexedCollation`) and matches each section by its whole title, then its first character, then its first character without accents; titles starting with a digit or symbol fall under #. `.custom([...])` takes your own entries.
- Entries without a section are dimmed by default and a touch on one lands on the nearest section after it (or before it at the end). `missing: .hidden` leaves them out. The letter bubble and the haptic only change when the landing section changes.
- When the rail is too short for every entry (landscape, accessibility text sizes), it shows evenly spaced entries with a dot between each, like the system index. The finger still maps proportionally along the whole column, so a dot lands on the letters it stands for and the bubble shows the real one.
- While a finger is down the rail keeps its touch-down geometry, pinned on screen, so a jump that resizes the scroll view (a large title collapsing) never moves letters under the finger. On release it moves into its new place at once, on a short spring with no overshoot that is most of the way there in about a tenth of a second.
- The letters sit on a clear liquid glass rail, always there, like the chrome of a tab bar. Touch down swells the rail a little either side of the letters, a signal glass bead buds out of the rail under the entry, and the letter bubble buds out of the rail beside it a beat behind: born as a drop inside the rail, it springs inward and opens to its full size, held to the rail by a liquid neck. While scrubbing the bead glides from entry to entry on a snap with a small give, the letter under it turning ink as it arrives, and the bubble rides a beat behind the bead, not the finger, its neck sliding along the rail. Its letter rolls in the direction of travel, blurring as it goes, without overshoot, like a counter. Scrubbing faster than about seven sections a second, or turning back, blurs the letter from old to new in place instead, so the bubble never smears.
- Dragging on past either end of the column (or further out than where the finger came down, when that was in the empty rail beyond the letters), the bubble gives a little with rubber-band resistance, never as far as the next row, stretching its neck. Only the drawing goes past: the section stays the first or last, so nothing scrolls or ticks. On release the bubble narrows to a drop and melts back into the rail at its letter, its tint draining as it goes, then the bead melts in after it and the rail settles; a bubble pulled past an end draws back to its letter on the way.
- A `.selection` haptic plays on touch down and on each new section, never on dimmed entries, repeated positions or the pull past an end.
- The drag lives on the rail itself, outside the scrolling content, so it never competes with the scroll view's pan; the scroll view keeps scrolling everywhere else. A system-cancelled touch resets the rail cleanly.
- VoiceOver: the rail is one adjustable element, "Section index", whose value is the current section. Swipe up or down to step through every section (including any the rail's entries do not reach); each step scrolls. Write `selection` from your own scroll tracking so it starts from the section on screen.
- Hardware keyboard: when the rail has focus, typing a letter jumps to it, arrow keys step between sections, and Home and End go to the first and last. The rail swells while focused, and each key buds the bead and the bubble out briefly before they melt home.
- Dynamic Type: letters, rows and the bubble scale up to the first accessibility size, then the rail collapses entries instead of growing; the bubble shows the section at display size while scrubbing. Over a dot of a collapsed rail the bead shrinks to stand for the dot.
- Right-to-left: the rail sits on the leading edge and the bubble buds out to its inner side.
- Reduce Motion: the bead and the bubble fade in and out where they rest instead of budding, the rail does not swell, the letter cross-fades instead of rolling, the bead and the bubble move between entries on a short spring with no overshoot, and there is no pull past the ends. The haptic and colors are unchanged. Before iOS 26 the rail, bead and bubble are frosted material with a light rim, and under Reduce Transparency they are solid.
- `Style` sets the `label`, `dimmed`, `bubble` (the bubble's and the bead's glass tint) and `ink` colors. `track` no longer does anything, since the rail is clear glass; it stays so existing code compiles. Every letter is semibold. `.disabled(true)` dims the rail and ignores touches.
