## Notes

- Two parts: `StretchHeader` goes first inside your `ScrollView` content, and `.stretchHeaderBar(title:progress:style:)` goes on the `ScrollView` inside a `NavigationStack`. Share one `@State` progress between them. The modifier hides the system bar background (and, on iOS 26, the system's soft scroll edge, which would haze the top of the hero), extends the scroll view under the bar, and draws its own solid bar in the style's `surface`, fading in with progress and gaining a soft shadow instead of a hairline.
- The title is display type: semibold, tight, 40 pt and scaled with Dynamic Type, with an optional uppercase `eyebrow` above it. Overscroll stretches the hero upward from its bottom edge, which stays joined to the row below, and the title grows with the pull, quickly at first and then easing toward 12%, the way the scroll view's own edge resists. Scrolling moves the hero at 70% speed inside its own slot, so neither its drawing nor its tap area hangs over the content below, while the title lags further and shrinks, sinking behind the hero's lower edge (or the pinned row) with the eyebrow fading as it goes. The inline title rises in over the last 20% of the collapse; each fade is eased, and the solid bar turns opaque gently as the pinned row arrives. Every value is a direct function of the scroll offset with no animation on top, so the header never trails the finger and the scroll view's own deceleration and bounce drive it. States: expanded, stretching, collapsing, collapsed.
- `Style`: `.standard` puts ink on a solid block hero with no scrim; `.overImage` uses a white title over a soft scrim for photos. Both set `surface`, the house ground behind the pinned row and the bar. Copy a style and change one property to customize.
- The `subtitle` row (stats, avatar, tabs) pins under the bar once the hero has scrolled past it and draws above the content that follows. For stats, `StretchHeaderStats(["5.4 mi", "1,000 ft up", "3.5 hours"])` draws each one on its own liquid glass pill, resting apart from the next, with the row's padding built in; two or three short stats fit a phone. Anything else you pass brings its own padding.
- Progress comes from `onScrollGeometryChange` on iOS 18 (fed through the modifier) and from the header's own position in the `.scrollView` coordinate space on iOS 17. `progress` itself stays linear in the offset; the bar eases its own fades from it. One soft haptic marks a deep pull.
- Reduce Motion drops the hero lag, the title parallax, the title's growth on a pull and the inline title's rise, but keeps the stretch, the collapse and every fade. The title then rides up with the hero instead of sinking, and the eyebrow, the title and the inline title fade over in turn, so the hand-off to the bar still reads. The hero reads as one header element and is hidden from VoiceOver once collapsed, when the inline title takes over. The stat pills are frosted material before iOS 26 and solid under Reduce Transparency.
- A full settings screen built around a header like this ships in SwiftPieces Pro (`settings-screen`).
