## Notes

- The photo is content; the controls around it are liquid glass. The aspect chips sit on one glass track with a red puck under the selected chip. Rotate and Reset rest joined, one pair of tools for the crop, apart from Cancel and the red Choose capsule. The zoom level and any notice float over the photo on dark glass, since the well is dark in both appearances. On iOS 26 this is Liquid Glass; before that, a frosted material; under Reduce Transparency, solid fills.
- Put it after a `PhotosPicker` (or the camera) in a `.fullScreenCover`, a pushed screen or a sheet. Keep the photo in state, for example `@State private var photo: UIImage?`, never created in `body`: a new image starts the cropper over.
- The photo always covers the frame. Zoom is relative to the smallest scale that fills the frame (1 to 5, less for very small photos so a crop never drops below 32 pixels), and the framing is stored as that zoom plus the photo point at the frame's centre. Rotating, switching aspect, rotating the device and resizing all keep the same subject in frame.
- Pinch zooms about the fingers while they pan, both at once, and the photo stays exactly under them. Past the edges of the photo, or past 1x and the maximum zoom, it follows with a scroll view's resistance. Let go and it lands: a flick carries on at the photo's own speed into a short coast, a pull past an edge springs back from where the band left it, and a pinch past a limit comes back from rest. None of it bounces or runs past its stop: a coast stops where it's going, and a hard flick into an edge arrives fast and stops at the edge. Touch the photo before it has landed, or while a double tap, a key step or Reset is still zooming or moving it, and it stops where it is, under the finger, the way a scroll view does; a change of aspect or a turn always runs to its end. At a zoom limit the frame's edge and corner marks flash red for an instant with a rigid tick. Double-tap toggles between filling the frame and 2.5x about the tapped point.
- A rule-of-thirds grid fades in the moment a finger touches the photo and out half a second after the last one lifts. The zoom level settles in above the frame on a glass capsule while pinching and lifts away with the grid.
- The aspect chips default to circle, square, 4:5, 16:9 and the photo's own shape; pass your own list (`[.square, .portrait, .ratio(3, 2)]`), retitle one with `.titled("Post")`, or pass a single aspect (or none) for a fixed frame. The red puck travels to the chip you pick as two tinted shapes, one landing with a little give and the other a beat behind, so it stretches along the track like a drop of liquid and gathers as it lands; the chip's picture turns solid ink on it. Meanwhile the frame and the photo morph to the new shape together without overshoot, so the photo never leaves part of the frame uncovered. `.circle` shows a round frame but the crop is square: clip it when you draw it.
- Rotate turns the photo a quarter to the left each tap; quick taps keep turning the same way. The turn never swings past its stop, and mid-turn the photo grows just enough that the frame's corners never leave it. `.original` turns with the photo.
- Chips, Rotate, Reset, Cancel and Choose sink a little under the finger and spring back. Reset's label dims while there is nothing to reset.
- Choose renders from the original, off the main actor, in the photo's own color space (Display P3 stays P3), upright, at up to `maxOutputDimension` (4096) on the longer side and never scaled up. `onCrop` gets a `Crop` with the `image`, the `rect` in pixels of the upright original and `quarterTurns` (to the left, applied after the rect), so a server can repeat the crop on the original, plus the `framing`. Choose spins until your `onCrop` returns, so you can await an upload there, then a check blurs in, and a beat later the label blurs back. The capsule holds its width throughout. If the cropper goes away mid-render the render is dropped; an `onCrop` that has started is never cut off.
- `framing:` binds the aspect, zoom, centre and rotation instead of `aspect:`. Store `crop.framing` (it is `Codable`) and pass it back to reopen a crop where it was left, or drive the cropper from your own controls. Reset returns to the photo filling the starting aspect and is enabled only once something changed.
- EXIF orientation is respected on screen and in the output. A photo that is much larger than the screen is shown from a copy cut once, off the main actor, so a 48 MP photo never decodes on the main thread; a quiet placeholder holds the frame meanwhile. A photo that can't be drawn shows "Couldn't open this photo" in the frame, and Choose and the puck drain to neutral glass. A failed render shows a short notice, rising in on glass at the bottom of the well, with an error haptic.
- A drag on the photo pans it: it never scrolls an enclosing scroll view or pulls a sheet down, because those pans wait for the cropper's own. Pinch and pan come from UIKit's recognizers, which report the fingers' moving centre.
- Haptics: a selection tick when the aspect changes, a light impact on rotate, a rigid tick at a zoom limit or an edge (once for a held key, not on every repeat), a soft tick on Reset, success when the crop is delivered, an error if rendering fails.
- VoiceOver: the photo is one adjustable element, "Crop area", with a value such as "2.5×, 4:5, Rotated left 90°". Swipe up and down to zoom, and use the Move up, down, left and right actions (a fifth of the frame each), Rotate left and Reset. Aspect chips are buttons with the selected trait ("4 to 5"), and rotations, resets and the finished crop are announced.
- Hardware keyboard: the arrow keys move and + and - zoom when the photo is focused, Return chooses, Escape cancels and Command-R rotates.
- Reduce Motion: the photo lands, zooms and changes aspect on a short spring with no overshoot, a quarter turn crossfades to the turned photo and frame instead of swinging round, and the puck fades over to the chip you pick instead of travelling. The grid, the zoom level, the photo appearing and the red limit flash still fade, so you can follow what changed, and pressed buttons dim instead of sinking.
- Dynamic Type: chips and buttons scale (chips stop at the second accessibility size so the photo keeps its room). When the buttons no longer fit on one line, Choose moves to its own full-width row, and at the largest sizes Cancel does too; separate actions never come closer than the merge distance, so their glass never necks. Right to left mirrors the toolbar; the photo is never mirrored. All text is one weight, semibold.
- `Style` sets the toolbar ground, the photo's well (dark in both appearances so the photo, scrim and frame read the same), the scrim, the frame's edge, the red accent (Choose, the puck and the limit flash) and the ink on it, labels, the well's corner radius (`0` runs the photo edge to edge) and the frame inset. `control` and `selectedControl` are no longer used: the controls are neutral glass and the puck takes `accent`. `Messages` replaces any of the copy, such as `choose: "Use photo"`.
