## Notes

- Put it after a `PhotosPicker` (or the camera) in a `.fullScreenCover`, a pushed screen or a sheet. Keep the photo in state, for example `@State private var photo: UIImage?`, never created in `body`: a new image starts the cropper over.
- The photo always covers the frame. Zoom is relative to the smallest scale that fills the frame (1 to 5, less for very small photos so a crop never drops below 32 pixels), and the framing is stored as that zoom plus the photo point at the frame's centre. Rotating, switching aspect, rotating the device and resizing all keep the same subject in frame.
- Pinch zooms about the fingers while they pan, both at once. Past the edges of the photo, or past 1x and the maximum zoom, it follows with a scroll view's resistance and springs back on release, with a short coast from a flick. At a zoom limit the frame's edge and corner marks flash red for an instant with a rigid tick. Double-tap toggles between filling the frame and 2.5x about the tapped point.
- A rule-of-thirds grid fades in while a finger is down and out half a second after the last one lifts. The zoom level shows above the frame while pinching.
- The aspect chips default to circle, square, 4:5, 16:9 and the photo's own shape; pass your own list (`[.square, .portrait, .ratio(3, 2)]`), retitle one with `.titled("Post")`, or pass a single aspect (or none) for a fixed frame. The selected chip's picture turns solid red. `.circle` shows a round frame but the crop is square: clip it when you draw it.
- Rotate turns the photo a quarter to the left each tap; quick taps keep turning the same way. Mid-turn the photo grows just enough that the frame's corners never leave it. `.original` turns with the photo.
- Choose renders from the original, off the main actor, in the photo's own color space (Display P3 stays P3), upright, at up to `maxOutputDimension` (4096) on the longer side and never scaled up. `onCrop` gets a `Crop` with the `image`, the `rect` in pixels of the upright original and `quarterTurns` (to the left, applied after the rect), so a server can repeat the crop on the original, plus the `framing`. Choose spins until your `onCrop` returns, so you can await an upload there, then shows a check. If the cropper goes away mid-render the render is dropped; an `onCrop` that has started is never cut off.
- `framing:` binds the aspect, zoom, centre and rotation instead of `aspect:`. Store `crop.framing` (it is `Codable`) and pass it back to reopen a crop where it was left, or drive the cropper from your own controls. Reset returns to the photo filling the starting aspect and is enabled only once something changed.
- EXIF orientation is respected on screen and in the output. A photo that is much larger than the screen is shown from a copy cut once, off the main actor, so a 48 MP photo never decodes on the main thread; a quiet placeholder holds the frame meanwhile. A photo that can't be drawn shows "Couldn't open this photo" in the frame and Choose turns neutral. A failed render shows a short notice with an error haptic.
- A drag on the photo pans it: it never scrolls an enclosing scroll view or pulls a sheet down, because those pans wait for the cropper's own. Pinch and pan come from UIKit's recognizers, which report the fingers' moving centre.
- Haptics: a selection tick when the aspect changes, a light impact on rotate, a rigid tick at a zoom limit or an edge, a soft tick on Reset, success when the crop is delivered, an error if rendering fails.
- VoiceOver: the photo is one adjustable element, "Crop area", with a value such as "2.5×, 4:5, Rotated left 90°". Swipe up and down to zoom, and use the Move up, down, left and right actions (a fifth of the frame each), Rotate left and Reset. Aspect chips are buttons with the selected trait ("4 to 5"), and rotations, resets and the finished crop are announced.
- Hardware keyboard: the arrow keys move and + and - zoom when the photo is focused, Return chooses, Escape cancels and Command-R rotates.
- Reduce Motion: the photo settles with a short ease instead of springs, rotation and the grid change without animating.
- Dynamic Type: chips and buttons scale (chips stop at the second accessibility size so the photo keeps its room). When the buttons no longer fit on one line, Choose moves to its own full-width row, and at the largest sizes Cancel does too. Right to left mirrors the toolbar; the photo is never mirrored.
- `Style` sets the toolbar ground, the photo's well (dark in both appearances so the photo, scrim and frame read the same), the scrim, the frame's edge, the red accent and the ink on it, the chip and button fills, labels, the well's corner radius (`0` runs the photo edge to edge) and the frame inset. `Messages` replaces any of the copy, such as `choose: "Use photo"`.
