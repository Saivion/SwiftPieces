## Notes

- One liquid glass control: a red play button joined to a glass capsule that holds the waveform and the time, and, when you pass `rate`, a smaller speed chip joined to the capsule's far end. Joined parts rest 4 points apart, so short liquid necks hold them together. Red appears only on the play button. On iOS 26 this is Liquid Glass; before that, a frosted material; under Reduce Transparency, solid fills.
- Playback is your app's. Pass your player's `time`, `duration` and `isPlaying`, start or pause it in `onPlayPause`, and move it in `onSeek`. The piece never runs a clock: between your updates the playhead glides on at the current speed for up to a second, so a periodic time observer or a timer that reports a few times a second still draws smooth progress, and a player that stops reporting never runs away.
- `samples` are loudness from 0 to 1, any count. They are resampled to the bars that fit the width (3 point bars, 2 points apart by default): the peak of each stretch when there are more samples than bars, smoothly in between when there are fewer. The loudest sample reaches full height and silence is a dot. Played bars are inked in the text colour and the rest stay muted, cut exactly at the playhead, mid-bar.
- Read `samples` from an audio file with `WaveformScrub.samples(from:count:)`: it decodes the file in chunks on a background thread (never the main actor), takes the peak of `count` equal stretches (160 by default) and scales them so the loudest is 1. Cancelling the calling task stops the read. Call it from `.task(id: url)` and store the result; it throws what `AVAudioFile` throws for a file it can't open.
- Touch the waveform and the playhead drops under the finger at once, the bars around it rise, and a glass bubble with the time buds up out of the capsule above the finger on a liquid neck and rides with it. Let go and the bubble melts home and `onSeek` fires once, with the time in seconds. A tap seeks too. Past either end the playhead stops at the start or the end with a soft tick. While a finger is down the `time` you pass is ignored, and after the seek the playhead holds where you let go until your `time` arrives near it (or 1.5 seconds pass), so it never jumps back while your player catches up.
- The bubble rises about 40 points above the control while a finger is down. Leave that room above it, or let it draw over what's there for that moment; at the very top of a scroll view, the scroll view's edge cuts it.
- Scrolling is never blocked. Touches come from a UIKit recognizer that begins on a sideways move and fails on a vertical one, and enclosing scroll views, sheets and the back swipe wait for it to fail. Inside a scroll view the press waits about a tenth of a second, so starting a scroll on the waveform never flashes the scrub.
- States: empty `samples` (the waveform is loading) shows a quiet row of dots with a soft light passing along them once a second (the dots hold still under Reduce Motion), and seeking still works. A `duration` of 0 (still unknown) turns seeking off. The time inside the capsule reads the total at the start and the elapsed time otherwise, in `0:42` form, or `1:12:30` from an hour; it keeps the width of the longest time, so the waveform never shifts. When the note has played to its end and stopped there, play turns to replay, and tapping it calls `onSeek(0)` and then `onPlayPause()`. Disabled (`.disabled(true)`), the control greys out, red included, and ignores touches.
- The speed chip cycles through `rates` (1×, 1.5× and 2× by default) on each tap, writing the `rate` binding with a selection tick, and its label morphs letter by letter. It keeps the width of the widest speed, so the waveform never moves, and its target is 44 points tall. Set your player's rate from the binding (`AVAudioPlayer.enableRate` must be on). Pass no `rate` for a plain voice note without the chip.
- Haptics: a light impact on play and pause, a selection tick when a finger grabs the waveform and when the speed changes, a soft tick when a scrub reaches the start or the end.
- VoiceOver: the play button reads Play, Pause or Play again. The waveform is one adjustable element labelled "Voice note" with a value such as "12 seconds of 42 seconds"; swipe up or down to move 5 seconds through `onSeek`. The speed chip is a button, "Playback speed", with a value such as "1.5 times" or "Normal". A two-finger double tap plays or pauses from anywhere on the control.
- Hardware keyboard: with the waveform focused, the arrow keys step 5 seconds (in reading order) and Space plays or pauses.
- Reduce Motion: the bars don't rise, the bubble fades in and out in place instead of budding, the glyph and digit changes cross-fade, and a seek while paused lands on a short spring with no overshoot.
- Dynamic Type: the text grows and the shapes follow it part way, up to 1.4 times, so the waveform keeps its room. The time inside the capsule and the speed stop growing at the largest standard size, the bubble at the first accessibility size. All text is one weight, semibold, with monospaced digits.
- Right to left: play stays on the leading side (now the right), the speed chip moves to the left, the bars play from the right and the time sits on the left of the capsule. The transport glyphs and the speed label are never mirrored.
- `Style` sets the play button's red and the glyph on it, the played and unplayed bars, the labels, the control's height (52 points, at least 44) and the bars' width and spacing. `Messages` replaces the VoiceOver labels, the speed label ("1.5×") and the position value ("12 seconds of 42 seconds"); every default goes through your String Catalog.
- Wire it to `AVAudioPlayer`: keep the player in an `@Observable` model, publish `currentTime` from a timer or `CADisplayLink` while playing, call `player.currentTime = seconds` in `onSeek`, set `player.rate` when `rate` changes, and set `isPlaying` false in `audioPlayerDidFinishPlaying`. For `AVPlayer`, read the time from `addPeriodicTimeObserver(forInterval:)` and seek with `seek(to:toleranceBefore: .zero, toleranceAfter: .zero)`.
