## Notes

- With `duration` set the strip runs itself: a `TimelineView(.animation)` draws the fill and a task sleeps for the rest of the segment, then advances `current`. Pausing (a hold, or `isPaused`) records the pause time and shifts the segment start on resume, so the fill continues exactly where it stopped.
- Without `duration` the strip is stateless like before: you own `current` and `progress`, and steps animate with a short linear curve so 30 Hz updates read as continuous.
- Holding anywhere on the surface for 200 ms pins the strip: the clock freezes on its frame and the bars dim at once with a soft tick. Releasing lifts the dim and resumes. A touch the system interrupts, such as an incoming call or Control Center, also lets go of the hold, so a story never stays paused. A shorter touch is a tap: the left 30% goes back (restarting the first segment when `duration` is set) and the rest advances, the moment the finger lifts.
- Segment steps fill or empty the bars on a short spring that never overshoots, since the bars say where the story is. The running fill is a clock: it is drawn from the time every frame and never animated, so it stays exact and never bounces.
- Haptics: a soft impact when a hold engages, and one `.selection` tick for each move you ask for with a tap or a VoiceOver swipe that changes something, including restarting the first segment when `duration` is set. A move with nothing to change stays silent: forward after the story has finished, or back on the first segment when you drive `progress`, since there is no clock to restart. The clock's own advances and changes you make to `current` stay silent too, since a story can run for minutes.
- `fillsContainer` stretches the invisible surface over the whole story with the bars pinned to the top; set it to false to use only the bar row.
- `onFinish` fires once when the last segment completes. VoiceOver reads "3 of 5", swipes up or down move between segments, and a custom action pauses or resumes.
- Holding buds a liquid glass "Paused" badge, tinted with the strip's `tint`, down out of the trailing end of the bars once the strip is pinned: it is born under the bars, springs out to its place, and its label sharpens just after. Let go and it rises back into the bars on a spring with no bounce, its label gone at once and its tint draining; the bars are not glass, so it thins away on the way and is gone by the time it gets there. A tap that moves back or forward flashes a glass chevron bubble in that zone, tinted the same way, which springs in with a little give, nudging in the direction of the move, and drifts on as it fades. Bars are 3 pt by default.
- With Reduce Motion the badge and the chevron fade in place instead of moving, and segment steps settle without overshoot. Under Reduce Transparency the glass draws solid. The dim, the haptics and the running fill are unchanged, so the strip still says when it is paused and where it is.
- `style:` (`StoryStrip.Style`) sets `barHeight`, `spacing`, `trackOpacity`, `inset`, `showsPausedBadge`, `showsTapHints`, and `ink` (the glyph and text color on the glass, which is tinted with `tint`). `.minimal` restores the version 1 look. All text is one weight, semibold.
- The example runs the strip in dark ink over solid color slides with one big idea each.
