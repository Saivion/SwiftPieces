## Notes

- The readout is display type: an uppercase meta line (range, point time, or held span), a semibold 44 pt figure with its decimals dimmed, and a liquid glass delta chip beside it, tinted sage for up and red for down with ink text, and left clear with muted figures when nothing changed.
- One finger drags to scrub: a rule and a butter dot sit on the nearest point, and a glass flag grows out of the top of the rule with its time. They move with the finger on the same frame and never ease after it, so the figure, the rule, the dot and the flag always agree, and `.selection` ticks at each point reached (not on release). Touching down brings the cursor in with the dot a beat ahead; letting go clears it and the flag sinks back into the rule. Holding still for 300 ms before moving pins the point under the finger as an anchor: the dot pops with a rigid tap and the drag becomes a range, a solid band fills the span and the figure shows the signed change with its percent. The range's change also buds out of the flag's side, on the side toward the middle of the plot, as a tinted glass chip that rests joined to the flag by a liquid neck; the flag keeps the pair inside the plot, and when the range ends the chip's tint drains and it melts back into the flag.
- Figures roll straight to their new value when the mode changes (touch, pin, release, range switch), never counting through the values between and never overshooting, and change at once while you scrub. The delta chip's glass around them resizes with a little give, and its arrow blurs across when the direction turns.
- Every series is normalized to 0...1 for drawing, so the y-scale never jumps between ranges. In `.line` mode datasets are resampled to 64 positions and switching ranges bends the line into the new one with no overshoot; the latest dot and the dashed baseline ride it, and the min and max labels leave at once and return once it lands. In `.bars` mode bars grow to their new heights and the active bar takes the accent block. New data in the same range morphs the same way without ending a scrub or a held range, and the min and max labels stay unless the min or max itself changed. When the chart goes from no data to data, on first appearance or when data loads later, the line unfolds from its baseline and bars rise from the floor, unhurried and without overshoot. A lazy list that rebuilds the row plays this again.
- At rest: no y-axis, one dashed baseline at the first value, monospaced min and max markers, the latest point as a butter dot, and three uppercase date labels whose format follows the span of the range. The picker is a row of glass segments, 44 pt tall, joined by liquid necks: segments press in lightly, and the one you pick swells and takes the accent tint, which flows to it through the necks with a little give while the one it leaves narrows and drains back to clear glass. A range set through `range` from outside moves it the same way.
- `tint` sets the line (the house text color by default). `Style` sets `accent` (also the picked segment's glass tint), `up` and `down` (the delta chips' glass tints), `ink`, `muted`, `band`, `ground` (the card the chart sits on), and `numeralSize`. `format` takes any `FormatStyle` from `Double` to `String`; deltas reuse it with a sign. `range` lets you own the picker index. An empty series shows "No data yet".
- VoiceOver gets one element with the latest value and change; swipe up or down steps the scrub point, and swiping up past the latest point lets it go.
- Under Reduce Motion the chart appears in place without unfolding, a range switch bends the line on a shorter spring, the picker's tint crossfades and its widths settle on a short spring with no give, the figures crossfade instead of rolling, the delta chip and the flag fade in without scaling, the range's change chip fades in beside the flag instead of budding, and a pinned anchor skips the pop but keeps its tap, band and readout change. Under Reduce Transparency the glass draws solid. Every string and symbol is semibold; size sets the hierarchy.
- A full dashboard screen ships in SwiftPieces Pro (`dashboard-screen`).
