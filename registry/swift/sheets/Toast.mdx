## Notes

- Single instance by design. Presenting again while visible does nothing. Every touch, release, action and new toast starts a fresh run of the countdown, and only the latest run can dismiss, so a timer left over from an earlier toast never closes a newer one.
- The toast is liquid glass, in three parts that rest joined: a glass card with the message and the detail, a round tile bubble at its leading end, and the action bubble at its trailing end, each held to the card by a liquid neck. The tile is tinted for its kind: `.success` sage, `.warning` and `.error` butter, and `.info` clear glass. All text is semibold.
- It arrives calmly from its edge, landing with a slight give, with the message already in place, so it can be read at once. As it settles the tile buds out of the card's leading end and then the action out of its trailing end, each pulling a neck as it springs to its place, with the kind's haptic (success, warning, error, or a soft tap for info). Both bubbles keep their place in the layout while they are inside the card, so the card never resizes. It leaves faster and firmer than it came, all in one piece, and slides all the way out past its edge. The presentation animation is scoped to the toast, so changes the host makes in the same transaction keep their own animation.
- `detail` adds a quieter second line. The action is a signal bubble: tapping it swaps the title for a check, pops the bubble with a light haptic, runs the handler, then dismisses a beat later. It still dismisses if the handler takes the view away first (switching tabs, pushing a screen), so the action never comes back live.
- The countdown is a `.task` on the toast and is cancelled the moment the toast leaves. Touching the toast pauses it: the thin bar freezes and the toast sinks toward your finger. Releasing resumes from where it stopped, and so does a touch the system interrupts, such as an incoming call.
- Swipe toward the edge to dismiss. The toast stays under your finger that way and gives with rubber-band resistance the other way. A swipe that would carry past 40 points leaves at the toast's own speed; anything shorter springs back at the speed you let go, with a little give past its resting place. Grabbing it again while it springs back takes it over from where it is, without a jump.
- On iOS 26 the parts are Liquid Glass and the necks between them are real; before that they are frosted glass with a light rim. With Reduce Transparency they are solid, so the toast reads the same over any content.
- With Reduce Motion the toast fades in and out, the bubbles fade in where they rest instead of budding, the press dims instead of sinking, and the action's pop is off. Haptics, the tile's tint and the check still say what happened.
- Placement reads the safe area of the view it is applied to: at the top it clears the Dynamic Island, at the bottom it rises with the keyboard. Apply the modifier to a view that does not ignore safe areas.
- Colors live in `Toast.Appearance` (passed as `appearance:`), because `Toast.Style` already names the kind. `surface` and `info` default to `.clear`, which leaves them neutral glass; any other color tints that glass.
- The message and detail are posted as a VoiceOver announcement on appear; the action button is a real button with its own label.
- For stacked, queued alerts with a notifications feed, see the Notifications screen (toast stack) in SwiftPieces Pro.
- **v2.3:** the countdown is a ring around the tile's glyph rather than a line under the card: it drains clockwise from the top on a faint track, holds while the toast is touched and once the action has run, and resumes with what is left. It is a clock, so it keeps running under Reduce Motion.
