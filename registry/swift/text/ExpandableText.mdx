## Notes

- SwiftUI has no API that reports truncation. The piece lays out three hidden copies at the paragraph's real width: the full text, the text at `lineLimit` lines, and the text at one line fewer. The "more" pill appears only when the full height is taller than the clamped height, so a short text never shows it.
- The copies re-measure whenever the width, Dynamic Type size, font or text changes (`onGeometryChange` on iOS 18, a `GeometryReader` on iOS 17). They never read the expanded state, so there is no layout loop.
- Until the first measurement lands, a plain `lineLimit` Text shows the same first lines, so first appearance does not jump.
- Collapsed, the full text is clipped to the clamped height. The end of the last line fades to transparent through a mask and the link sits in the faded space as a small liquid glass pill reading "more", centred on the line. The mask needs no background color, so it works over cards, images and materials. Set `Style.fade` to paint a gradient into a solid color instead.
- Expanding animates the clip height to the full height. Lines are laid out once at full length, so nothing reflows or jumps. The text unfurls on a calm spring with no visible overshoot and tucks back a little quicker. The pill rides the text's bottom edge on the same curve: down from the last line to hang just under the text as it opens, its label morphing letter by letter from "more" to "less", and back up as it closes. The fade at the end of the last line fills back in as the pill leaves it and returns a beat into the collapse, once the fold has passed most of the lines below it. This holds whether the piece is pinned at the top, the bottom or the center. With `showsLess: false` the paragraph stays open, and the pill sinks away once it does.
- The pill is neutral glass, so it stays quiet inside reading text; its label is `Style.link`. On iOS 26 it is Liquid Glass and swells under a press; before iOS 26 it is frosted; under Reduce Transparency it is an opaque fill. The labels are localized keys, so the morph is `Text`'s own letter-by-letter content transition.
- Taps on the pill, the body and the VoiceOver actions toggle inside one animation, so a card or row around the piece grows and shrinks with the text. If you drive `isExpanded` yourself, change it inside `withAnimation` and the text folds on your animation together with its container; changed without one, the paragraph still folds on its own curve but its container jumps to the new height. The faded line end keeps its own timing either way, tuned to the piece's collapse.
- Font, color and alignment come from the environment, like `Text`: `.font`, `.foregroundStyle`, `.multilineTextAlignment`.
- `AttributedString` keeps bold, italics and links. With `togglesOnTap`, tapping the body toggles too; a tap that opens a link does not toggle, and lines hidden below the fold have no hit area. Links open through your `openURL` environment action.
- `isExpanded` lets you drive or observe the state, for example to keep one review open at a time.
- RTL: the fade and the pill sit on the trailing side, which is the left in right-to-left languages.
- `Style` sets the pill's label color and weight (semibold, the house weight, by default), fade width (scaled with Dynamic Type) and the optional solid fade color. `.disabled(true)` dims the pill's label and stops toggling. All of the piece's type is semibold.
- Reduce Motion: the text opens in a 0.2 second ease and closes in 0.18 seconds, with no spring. The pill still moves with the bottom edge of the text and its label cross-fades; the fade waits until the collapse is landing.
- VoiceOver reads the full text in both states, with links in the rotor. "Show more" and "Show less" actions resize the paragraph for sighted VoiceOver users; the visual pill is hidden from VoiceOver.
- The pill draws at one line tall but has a hit area of at least 44 by 44 points. There are no haptics: this is reading content.
