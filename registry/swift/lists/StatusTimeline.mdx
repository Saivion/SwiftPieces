## Notes

- Every node is a liquid glass bubble, tinted by its status: the live step signal (`style.current`) with a breathing ring, completed steps sage with a drawn check, a failed step butter with an x, and pending steps clear glass with a quiet number. On iOS 26 this is Liquid Glass; before that frosted Material, and under Reduce Transparency solid fills.
- An open step's detail sits on a glass panel that buds out of its node: born inside the node as a small round blob, it grows out into the panel behind the step's text and rests joined to the node by a liquid neck, so the node and its detail read as one shape. The live step's panel takes the signal tint with dark ink, a failed step's the butter tint, any other step's stays clear glass. Closing, the detail blurs away, the panel's tint drains and it melts back into the node.
- Layout is nodes leading, content trailing: a 44pt column holds the 30pt node and the connector below it, which stretches to the row's own height, so long titles and expanded detail never break the line. Rows hug their content, so the timeline never stretches to fill a tall container. Each row's glass is its own liquid group, so a node holds its own panel and never melts into its neighbour.
- A status change plays as a relay down the trail. The step that completes stamps first: its number blurs out, its node takes the sage tint, the check draws a beat later, and as it lands the node pops with the `.success` haptic. Its open panel drains and melts back into the node. The ink then runs down its connector in the text color, on a fixed 0.4s clock, and the next step takes its new status only as the ink reaches it: its node lights signal, and with `expandsCurrent` its panel buds out a beat after that.
- A `.failed` step lands the same way: its node turns butter with an x, gives a small shake with the `.error` haptic on the same beat, and the connector below it turns dashed, so the trail visibly stops. A reset or any change that doesn't complete a step lands in one beat, with checks and ink clearing quickly.
- A change that completes three or more steps at once is catching up rather than handing off, so it lands in one beat. Steps are tracked by title: a step that moves still relays its change, a renamed one shows its status at once, and a repeated title keeps its own state. A newer change mid-relay carries on from what is on screen, and a stamp it cuts short still plays its haptic once. Each change plays each haptic once. A timeline that appears shows its current state without replaying anything, its open panel already out, and one that changed while off screen catches up as it returns, its tints, checks and ink settling together with no relay and no haptics. VoiceOver reads every step's real status at once, never the relay's delayed drawing of it.
- Tap any step with `detail` to expand it; one step is expanded at a time. The row grows around the detail on a calm spring while the panel buds out and the text blurs in, clipped to the panel so it never shows past its edge; closing is quicker. `expandsCurrent` opens the current step on appearance and follows it as the live step moves on, including a move made while the timeline was off screen, which it shows as it stands on return.
- `Style` (`StatusTimeline.Style`): `current`, `complete`, `failed`, `ink`, `text`, `muted`. `surface` is kept so existing code compiles but no longer draws anything: open detail sits on glass. `tint:` still overrides both the current and complete colors.
- Under Reduce Motion every change lands in one short beat with no relay: tints, checks, ink and detail crossfade without drawing, popping or shaking, panels fade in place instead of budding, and the rows reflow once. The breathing ring becomes a still halo that breathes in opacity, so the live step still reads as in progress. The colors, the marks and both haptics still carry each change. Each step is a single VoiceOver element that reads its title, timestamp and status, and is a button only when it has detail.
