## Notes

- Snapping uses `.scrollTargetBehavior(.viewAligned)` with content margins sized so the aligned page is centered; the first and last pages center too. Fractional progress is read from the page row's position in a coordinate space named on the scroll view itself (the built-in `.scrollView` space starts inside the content margins), so it lands on whole pages and works on iOS 17 without `onScrollGeometryChange`.
- Depth is driven by the same progress: pages shrink by `style.recede`, fade by `style.dim`, and their shadow gets lighter and leans back toward the centered page as they leave center. The `phase` handed to your content is -1...1, for inner parallax; the example drifts a cropped ink disc by `phase * 40` inside a solid color card. Under Reduce Motion the phase is 0 and only the dim remains.
- Hold a neighbor page and it starts forward, about a third of the way to center; tap it and it comes the rest of the way on the snap spring that every tap, scrub and VoiceOver page move uses. A swipe that starts on a neighbor scrolls as usual and cancels the press. The centered page keeps its own gestures.
- The pages stay your content; the indicator under them is liquid glass. A "01 / 04" counter (current page in rounded semibold, total dimmed, with a short roll and no overshoot as it changes) sits on a glass capsule joined by a liquid neck to the glass track that carries the dots, so the two read as one control, centered under the pages. The current page is a pill of tinted glass (`style.indicatorActive`) riding inside the track. It is drawn in absolute page coordinates, as a pure function of the scroll position, so it never lags the pages and never jumps when one settles. Between two dots its leading edge reaches the next dot first and its trailing edge follows, so it stretches by up to half the dot spacing and gathers in as the page lands. Past the first or last page it flattens against the end dot as the scroll view rubber-bands, and springs back with it. On iOS 26 this is Liquid Glass; before that frosted Material, and under Reduce Transparency solid fills.
- While you drag the pages or touch the track, the dots dim and the pill widens, on one press spring. They settle back on the snap spring as soon as you let go of the track, or once dragged pages come to rest, so after a fling they stay active until the pages land. The whole 44pt-tall track is scrubbable: the pill glides onto the finger in about 0.1s while following its movement 1:1, flattening against the ends if you pull past them, while the pages follow on the snap spring, with a selection haptic per page. On release the pill eases back to the page position; touch the track again before it gets there and it is picked up from where it is drawn, without a jump.
- `Style` (`DepthCarousel.Style`, also `DepthCarouselStyle`): `recede`, `dim`, `shadowOpacity`, `indicatorActive`, `indicatorInactive`, `showsCounter`. With `showsCounter` off the track stands alone.
- Reduce Motion: pages don't scale or hand out a phase, and keep their dim. The pill slides rigidly without stretching, stops at the ends and doesn't widen; the dots still dim. A held neighbor shades slightly (darker in light mode, lighter in dark) instead of moving forward. The counter fades instead of rolling, and page moves land on a short spring with no overshoot. Native paging and its fling are unchanged.
- VoiceOver treats the carousel as one container that reads "Page x of y" and supports swipe up/down to change pages; the indicator is hidden from it.
- `scrollClipDisabled()` lets neighbor pages peek past the carousel's own bounds, so give it full width.
- Want blur-and-tone depth, pinch peek-zoom and a full album screen? That is the Depth Gallery screen in SwiftPieces Pro.
